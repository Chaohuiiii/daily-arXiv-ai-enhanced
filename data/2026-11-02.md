Abstract: Unlike conventional architectures that treat memory as static pattern storage, the hippocampus organizes experience as ordered sequences and retrieves them through temporally compressed replay spanning prior, current, and anticipated states. This capacity of context-appropriate sequence prediction remains absent from conventional deep learning accelerators and existing neuromorphic fabrics. We present HippocamFLEX, a fully synthesizable digital implementation of these hippocampal computational principles, realized with SystemVerilog RTL on the AMD Zynq UltraScale+,XCZU9EG. The design maps the hippocampal trisynaptic pathway onto three pipeline stages: a Dentate Gyrus / Entorhinal Cortex (DG/EC) stage for hypervector proximity matching, a CA3 stage for context-gated recurrent sequence prediction with multi-phase temporal tagging, and a CA1 stage that integrates past, present, and predicted future events into a theta-sequence inspired output. While much brain-inspired hardware relies on analog mixed-signal design for energy efficiency, this work targets FPGA prototyping as a deliberate architectural choice: FPGAs offer rapid design-space exploration without tape-out cost, bit-exact determinism that simplifies verification. The design supports 64 events across 16 contexts and is verified on physical silicon across context-isolation, temporal-ordering, and mid-sequence sliding-window test cases and is inherently scalable to larger event and context sets.

Abstract: The electronic structure of molecules is foundational for scientific advancement, however determining it remains challenging due to the growth of the molecular Hilbert space with system size. Recent approaches address this by using quantum circuits to sample candidate configurations and sample-based quantum diagonalization (SQD) to compute the ground state. We propose ZenKO, a novel gradient-based, iterative SQD method that employs a hybrid quantum-classical approach. ZenKO decomposes configuration selection into discovery, validation, and refinement, enabling targeted subspace expansion and guided exploration. Our evaluation demonstrates that, across $H_2O$, $H_{10}$, and $N_2$ molecules, ZenKO improves ground-state accuracy compared to prior iterative methods, while significantly reducing classical cost.

Abstract: Morphogenesis of diatom silica shells involves a sequence of topological transitions: initially separate ribs grow and branch, then merge through bridges, leaving a regular network of pores. Existing models based on diffusion-limited aggregation or reaction-diffusion reproduce either branching or pore formation, but not both within a single framework. Here we propose a two-stage model of diffusion-driven morphogenesis with evolving connectivity. The model is formulated on a fixed finite element mesh with a density field on an active layer. Stage 1 reproduces dendritic growth, where a stimulus based on the local diffusive flux exceeds a threshold. Stage 2 uses an inverted stimulus, where high local flux suppresses precipitation, stabilizing pores between the dendrites. The update rules are analogous to sensitivity in density-based topology optimization and to strain energy density in bone remodeling. The model predicts distinct morphological regimes on each stage, controlled by the respective thresholds. For a reference calculation, the predicted pore network is regular, with approximately uniform spacing, and pores are absent near the domain edges, consistent with observations on diatom valves. The model provides a unified framework for describing both stages of silica morphogenesis and suggests a route for comparing computational predictions with cryo-electron tomography data.
