Abstract: Programmable receivers allow an optimal performance in the variable conditions of the wireless channel. An extensive analysis of their noise-power trade-off enables the development of a main receiver with embedded wake-up circuitry. This makes the seamless integration of wake-up receivers (WuRXs) in RF receivers possible, avoiding power, area, complexity, and cost overheads in the system. In this paper, we propose the first LoRa-compatible receiver with an integrated wake-up mode and a programmable noise-power trade-off. In main-receiver mode, it consumes 4.57 mW for a sensitivity of -138 dBm, but it can save up to 62.6% of power consumption at the cost of an 8.3-dB sensitivity degradation. In wake-up mode, sensitivity down to -126 dBm is achieved for a 32-ms latency and 1.5 mW of power consumption. The power consumption remains relatively high due to a sub-optimal design of the the phase-locked loop (PLL). In the end, the wake-up mode consumes 3X less than the lowest-noise configuration in main-receiver mode.
